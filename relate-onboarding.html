01 — Background / About the App
What Project Relate does
Project Relate is a Google Research app that uses machine learning to help people with non-standard or atypical speech communicate more easily and independently, while giving them access to Google Assistant. Users train a personalized speech recognition model by recording a set of phrases; once trained, the app can transcribe their speech, read it aloud in a synthesized voice, and connect them to voice-activated tasks.

02 — Problem
A funnel that collapsed after signup
Funnel data revealed steep drop-off after signup: only about 20% of invited users ever opened the app, and just 3% became 30-day active users. The steepest declines happened right after users started the app and were asked to record phrases, and again once they had recorded enough samples to build a personalized model. Many users simply didn’t understand what they would get in return for the effort.

03 — Challenge
Built without UX — value never surfaced
Project Relate had originally been built by engineers without dedicated UX support, so it lacked visual polish and a clear way to communicate its value. Users were asked to record 500 phrases with no explanation of why, leaving them confused, discouraged, and prone to abandoning the app before ever experiencing its benefits.

04 — Solution
Three pillars for first-run
I designed and shipped a new onboarding sequence built around three pillars: feature education, step-by-step onboarding, and re-teaching. Together, these clarified the app’s purpose up front, guided users through the recording process with contextual tooltips, and re-engaged them with a refresher once their personalized speech model was ready.

My Design Process
Kickoff, journey mapping, research and iterations. Expand for full story
06a — Project kickoff
Understanding the speech experience
I led a cross-functional brainstorming workshop to align the team on user needs across three impairment groups: cognition, motor, and hearing. Using a “how might we” prompt for each group, the team generated and clustered ideas on an affinity map, giving us a shared direction before any design work began.
Kickoff synthesis — user goals and focus areas
User stories — jobs to be done at first-run
How might we — cognition, motor, and hearing
06b — User journey
Aligning the team on friction
I mapped the end-to-end journey from download to receiving a personalized speech model, documenting tasks, emotions, and opportunities at each stage. Sentiment dropped sharply during the lengthy recording process and recovered once users received their model — pinpointing exactly where onboarding support was most needed.

06c — Research
Finding the real friction
A heuristic evaluation flagged key improvement areas. User interviews confirmed users were confused about features and personalization, not just “unmotivated.” Funnel analysis also showed the two biggest drop-offs occurred right after app start and after completing enough recordings to qualify for a personalized model.

06d — Design iterations
Education, pacing, and refinement
Based on the research, I framed four “how might we” directions that became three design pillars: feature education, onboarding, and re-teach. I iterated on low-fi wireframes with a UX writer and UX expert to tighten copy, tooltip sequencing, and pagination, and to address edge cases from research.

06e — Final design
Worth the wait — then ship
When the personalized model was ready, a re-education moment closed the training-to-payoff gap — with wait-time expectations set upfront. Internal testing produced consistently positive testimonials.
Next steps
  • Partner with marketing on a GM3 campaign for new and existing users
  • Run usability testing against the GM2 baseline
Lessons learned
  • State product value early — purpose before effort
  • Design for edge cases and cognitive diversity from the start
  • Keep onboarding step-by-step so users can build a mental model
